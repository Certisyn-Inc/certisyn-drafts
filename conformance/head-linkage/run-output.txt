construction, from the section text and nothing else
  Prior-Entry Hash   the Signing Input Digest of the preceding entry's
                     Entry Signature; thirty-two zero octets in entry 1
  Self-Entry Hash    the digest over the entry array with the Self-Entry
                     Hash and Entry Signature positions encoded as CBOR
                     null, under RFC 8949 Section 4.2.1
  Entry Signature    over the array with the Entry Signature position
                     alone null, so it covers the Self-Entry Hash
  protected header   the algorithm identifier (label 1) and the key
                     identifier (label 4), which the draft requires
                     there and forbids in an unprotected header. No
                     digest identifier is carried in either header here,
                     or anywhere in the draft, whose upgrade path
                     declares three primitive classes and no digest one.
  fields=linkage     Entry Sequence Number, Prior-Entry Hash, Self-Entry
                     Hash, Signing Input Digest. The three hashes cannot
                     be recomputed by a holder of the array: it carries
                     neither the payload nor the protected header.

scenario
  witness Statement  covers entry 4, obtained from the witness's own
                     origin, as the draft requires and as every row

  response names     the service's head at entry 2
  reader holds       linkage arrays for entries 2 to 4, read from the
                     service, which is where the draft has them read
  keys               one sealing key and one witness key throughout

1. THE CONDITION AS THE DRAFT STATES IT

   Three parts, verified together. That the Self-Entry Hash of the array
   at the Statement's sequence number equals item 3 of that Statement.
   That the Self-Entry Hash of the array at the named head equals the one
   the response names. That the array at each sequence number carries, as
   its Prior-Entry Hash, the Signing Input Digest the array below it
   reports. The draft forbids testing a Self-Entry Hash against a
   Prior-Entry Hash, which are digests over different preimages, and no
   such test is made.

   case                                    quorum
   --------------------------------------------------------
   A   served honestly                     accept
   B   digest moved whole                  accept
   B'  digest moved, witness before        refuse
   C   rewritten, served plainly           refuse
   D   rewritten, witness value spliced    accept
   D2  the same, top fourth value too      accept
   D3  the same, whole top array           accept
   E   response names another head         refuse

   reasons given on refusal
     B'  digest moved, witness before
       Self-Entry Hash at entry 4 does not equal item 3 of the Statement
     C   rewritten, served plainly
       Self-Entry Hash at entry 4 does not equal item 3 of the Statement
     E   response names another head
       Self-Entry Hash at entry 2 is not the one the response names

   E is the control for the second part, which every other row satisfies.

2. THE RESULT

   An entry's Signing Input Digest is bound to that entry's Self-Entry
   Hash. The Entry Signature nulls its own position alone, so its payload
   carries the Self-Entry Hash, and the Signing Input Digest is taken
   over the Sig_structure around that payload. The binding is real and it
   is in the entry.

   A holder of a linkage array cannot check it. Verifying it needs the
   payload and the protected header, and the projection carries neither.
   The three hashes arrive in one array, bound to one another, and are
   checked apart.

   The condition anchors two values to something outside the service: the
   Self-Entry Hash at the top of the span, which a witness signed, and
   the Self-Entry Hash at the bottom, which the response names. The chain
   it then walks runs on the Prior-Entry Hash and the Signing Input
   Digest. Those are different positions. Neither anchored value is in a
   position the chain reads, and no value the chain reads is anchored.

   Row D is what follows. The service holds a rewritten history, serves
   the Self-Entry Hash the witness published at the top of the span, and
   serves its own values everywhere else. Every part of the condition
   holds. The Statement is untouched and is obtained from the witness's
   own origin, as the draft requires; the service supplies only the
   arrays and the response, which is what the draft has it supply.

   Entry 4 as the projection delivers it, honestly in A, rewritten in C,
   and as the service serves it in D:

     Entry Sequence Number  A  4

   D takes its third value from A and the rest from C. The witness
   signature is satisfied and pins one octet string that nothing the
   reader walks connects to. The chain the reader walks ends at the top
   array's Prior-Entry Hash, which the service chose, and the head the
   response names is an entry of C.

   D2 and D3 are the same construction with more of A copied in, and they
   are here because they are what the obvious repairs run into. Section 4
   takes them.

3. WHICH POSITIONS THE CONDITION BINDS

   Each of the nine hash positions in the span, replaced in turn with a
   value the service chose, against the honest ledger and the honest
   Statement and response of row A.

   entry         position                verdict
   --------------------------------------------------------
   2             Prior-Entry Hash        accept
   2             Self-Entry Hash         refuse
   2             Signing Input Digest    refuse
   3             Prior-Entry Hash        refuse
   3             Self-Entry Hash         accept
   3             Signing Input Digest    refuse
   4             Prior-Entry Hash        refuse
   4             Self-Entry Hash         refuse
   4             Signing Input Digest    accept

   Three positions are free:
     entry 2 Prior-Entry Hash
     entry 3 Self-Entry Hash
     entry 4 Signing Input Digest

   Two of the three are ends: the bottom array's Prior-Entry Hash reaches
   below the span and the top array's Signing Input Digest reaches above
   it, and the condition spans neither. The third is not an end. Every
   interior Self-Entry Hash is free, and a relying party that goes on to
   use one, to check a receipt against a particular entry, is using a
   value this condition never constrained. The text does not say so.

   The reader also recomputes no digest over ledger content. Arbitrary
   octets, digests of nothing in any ledger, chain as readily as an
   honest span:

     arbitrary octets, a witness signing the top of them
       accept | 2 links chained

   Row B follows from that and is not a separate finding. A deployment
   that moved its digest whole satisfies the condition because the
   condition compares octets and never asks what produced them. The
   draft fixes SHA-256, so such a deployment is non-conformant rather
   than adversarial, and a reader holding whole entries refuses it. That
   reader, run over each ledger under SHA-256:

     A    accept  4 entries recompute and verify under sha256
     B    refuse  entry 1 Self-Entry Hash does not recompute under sha256
     C    accept  4 entries recompute and verify under sha256

   It accepts C, which is the rewritten history, because a rewrite the
   operator re-sealed is internally perfect. Nothing a reader recomputes
   from the entries alone separates it. What row B measures is the
   projection's expressiveness: no
   value in it names the digest, so nothing carried in a protected
   header, where a digest identifier would naturally go, reaches this
   reader at all.

4. WHAT CLOSES IT

   pinned    the Statement carries a sixth item, the Signing Input Digest
             of the entry it covers, and the reader checks the top
             array's fourth value against it.
   ends      the Statement carries a seventh item, the Prior-Entry Hash
             of that entry, and the response names the Signing Input
             Digest at the head it names, so that both ends of the span
             are anchored in positions the chain does read.
   attested  the witness publishes the linkage arrays for the span it
             observed, signed and served from its own origin, and the
             reader requires the served arrays to be those arrays.

   case                                  rule     pinned   ends    attested
   ------------------------------------------------------------------------
   A   served honestly                   accept   accept   accept  accept
   B   digest moved whole                accept   accept   accept  accept
   B'  digest moved, witness before      refuse   refuse   refuse  refuse
   C   rewritten, served plainly         refuse   refuse   refuse  refuse
   D   rewritten, witness value spliced  accept   refuse   refuse  refuse
   D2  the same, top fourth value too    accept   accept   refuse  refuse
   D3  the same, whole top array         accept   accept   accept  refuse
   E   response names another head       refuse   refuse   refuse  refuse

   Pinning closes D and D2 defeats it, at the cost to the service of one
   further octet string the witness already published. Anchoring both
   ends closes D2 and D3 defeats it, because the array between the two
   ends is the service's and it can be made to join an anchored top to an
   unanchored bottom. Lengthening the span makes that easier and never
   harder: every interior array is two more values the condition
   constrains only against each other.

   That is the shape of the thing. Anchoring endpoints cannot work, at
   any span, because the chain is a sequence of equalities among values
   the reader cannot recompute, and an equality between two values the
   same party supplies is not evidence. Soundness has to come from a copy
   of the chain the service did not write.

   The witness holds the entries it observed, so it can publish the
   arrays. It already publishes a signed artefact at its own origin and
   the draft already forbids taking that artefact from the responding
   service, so the channel, the origin rule and the trust boundary all
   exist. What is added is the arrays themselves. That is the attested
   column, and it refuses every row where the service departed from what
   a witness saw.

   The alternative that needs nothing new is the first branch of the same
   condition, where a Statement covers the head the response names
   exactly. It carries no span, so it has no interior, and the draft
   already says of it that there is nothing to link.

   On the relationship to deployed practice. An independent party that
   signs and serves a bounded, floored, addressable interval of a log's
   contents from its own origin is the mirror role, specified at C2SP
   tlog-mirror and deployed. Nothing here claims it. A mirror assumes a
   Merkle log and carries subtree consistency proofs, and neither is
   available over a plain hash chain with no skip links and no history
   tree, which is the case measured above.

5. AN ENCODING NOTE, MEASURED IN PASSING

   The Self-Entry Hash is taken over an encoding the draft requires to
   satisfy Section 4.2.1 of RFC 8949. An implementer of that requirement
   who reaches for cbor2's canonical=True gets Section 4.2.3 map key
   ordering instead: length-first, ties broken bytewise.

     map                        {100000: 1, 'z': 2}
     cbor2 canonical=True       a2617a021a000186a001
     RFC 8949 Section 4.2.1     a21a000186a001617a02
     SHA-256 of the first       aa5fd77249529a52834ddfc60b8b21517012acb8b761b05e934d85f93c5f072a
     SHA-256 of the second      461464febd51d665994a663c20fe7b4e6579448c02586508e2200435a43a1921

   The call is right in every other respect, and one of those respects
   matters more than the ordering. Preferred serialization requires the
   shortest floating-point form that preserves the value, and
   canonical=True gives it: 1.5 encodes as f93e00. This harness
   delegates every leaf to that call for exactly that reason and takes
   back the map ordering alone. An implementer who replaces the call
   outright rather than wrapping it trades one departure for another.

   Two maps sit on the digest path here: each protected header, inside
   every Signing Input Digest. Their keys are COSE labels 1 and 4, both
   one byte, so the two orderings agree and nothing above turns on the
   difference. A protected header mixing integer and text-string labels
   would not agree, and neither would a type-specific field that is a map
   with keys of differing encoded length. Where that holds, two
   implementations compute two Self-Entry Hashes over one entry, and the
   head comparison treats it as a fork. A sentence naming the section and
   naming the ordering it is not would close it.
